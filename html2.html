Field notes
Event horizonr = rₛ = 2GM/c². The point of no return — not a surface, just the radius where escape velocity equals c.
Photon spherer = 1.5 rₛ. Light can (unstably) orbit here in a circle — its glow forms the bright ring you see silhouetting the horizon.
ISCOr = 3 rₛ. Innermost stable circular orbit — matter can't stay in a circular orbit any closer, so it marks the accretion disk's inner edge.

The probe crosses the horizon in finite time by its own clock — but from out here, its light redshifts and dims, and you never actually see it cross. Both are correct.
What this is: for every pixel, a light ray is integrated backward through the exact Schwarzschild photon-orbit equation d²u/dφ² + u = (3/2)rₛu² (u = 1/r) via RK4 — the lensing, the photon ring, and the disk's warped far edge are not painted on, they fall out of that integration. Disk brightness combines Doppler beaming from Keplerian orbital speed with gravitational redshift √(1−rₛ/r). What this isn't: the disk temperature/color mapping is a simplified blackbody gradient rather than a full radiative-transfer solve, and this renders a non-rotating (Schwarzschild) hole — a spinning Kerr hole would warp its photon ring asymmetrically.
